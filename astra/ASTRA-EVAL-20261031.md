RAN. The algebra specified at `SYNTHESIS:105–137` does not have the claimed free object. The distance is smaller than inventing a new semantics, but larger than composing the current headline lemmas. `Open.resubst_wf` (`Slot/Proofs/Protocol.lean:459`) preserves well-formedness, history and its obligation bound under the guard. `lost_reply_blocks_replan` (`Slot/Proofs/Negotiation.lean:155`) rules out switching provider or cancelling after dispatch; `replan_before_dispatch` at line 236 admits the corresponding earlier change. `admitOp_sound` and `commit_once` (`Slot/Proofs/Protocol.lean:527`, 539) supply admission and one-step durable spending facts. These are valuable ingredients. But `Open.WF` (`Slot/Proofs/ProtocolTyping.lean:114`) has no current view, while `Handler.handle` and `interpret` (`Slot/Protocol.lean:190–209`) have no admission guard. `commit_once` talks about the resulting view; it does not itself ensure arbitrary subsequent world updates cannot restore the token.

RAN. That separation is executable. In the negotiation fixture, with every required fact false and the capability live, `admitOp spec withdrawnView (Op.commit .p1)` evaluates to false. Independently, `interpret (env true) (plan .p1) 0` finishes with `(delivered=true, calls=1)`. The reference handler is allowed to do this; I am not reporting a violation of its specified contract. I am reporting that the currently assembled library is not yet an authorization-enforcing runner. A theorem that calls this execution “admitted” without a connecting hypothesis would be false.

PROPOSED. Build that connection in three substantial pieces: the small guarded dispatch/response/replan transition system and its reachable-state invariant; a concrete history adapter preserving spent tokens and version/retirement facts; and a running negotiation that exercises revocation before dispatch, an alternative selection, then a lost reply with attempted replacement refused. State the observation/refinement boundary to the host explicitly, including its serialization/durable-commit assumption. The abstract invariant should require no new Core4 constructor, solver or general category library. Connecting actual emitted Core4 plans and inherited laws to that runner is the next bridge, and a real distributed host adds its own obligations. I would not promise this complete end-to-end result in a single day. The current pieces justify a focused integration project, not another foundational rewrite.

INFERRED. The semiring trace theorem is closer, but weaker: a completed run used candidates whose supports held. It largely follows by accumulating existing local success lemmas. It is worth proving as an internal invariant, especially if it produces usable explanations, but it leaves the dispatch boundary untouched. The algebraic-subtyping soundness theorem is also worthwhile, yet most of its conceptual story is established prior art unless this project's resource and row extension is proved and used. The replanning theorem has the best ratio of distinctive behavior to missing machinery, and it answers the practical question the rest of the design keeps approaching: what may change after a reason disappears, and what must remain because an action has already left the machine?

PROPOSED. My immediate order is therefore executable preparation and frontend/checker convergence; the narrow adapter experiment in parallel with that; the guarded runner and replan theorem; then provenance as a refinement that improves explanations and dependency reuse. Keep the semiring experiment small until it survives the countermodels. Keep the stronger type-inference project, but make its first deliverable a checked, usable template with fewer required annotations rather than an imported lattice vocabulary. The design deserves to become a system whose user can safely change their mind. The last two days built much of the necessary evidence; the next work should connect it to the action that evidence permits.
