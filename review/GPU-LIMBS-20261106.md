# Portable Goldilocks limbs (2026-11-06)

The GPU scalar carrier is a little-endian pair of u32 words, emitted as
`vec2<u32>`. All carry chains, shifts and reduction operate on u32 values.
The executable reference proves extended multiplication via 16-bit partial products;
the emitted `u32_mul_wide` uses a bounded widening multiply with explicitly u32 operands,
then extracts the low/high words. RADV emits native `v_mul_lo_u32` and `v_mul_hi_u32`.
This relies on Naga/wgpu's SHADER_INT64 extension to express a 32×32→64 primitive;
it is not standard WGSL u32-only syntax. General word multiplication uses that exact
low-limb product plus two truncated u32 cross products, with no general 64×64 shader multiply.


## Arithmetic proof chain

`Obend.Hardware.Gpu.Limbs` defines actual `BitVec 32` operations, not an
unbounded-integer implementation decorated with a correctness condition.
`Word.val` is their mathematical interpretation and `Word.bits` their connection
to the existing `BitVec 64` scalar semantics.

- `mul32Nat_sound` establishes the partial-product identity. `mul32Nat_bounds`
  bounds both result limbs. `mul32_refines` checks the actual u32 operations
  against that schedule, including the intermediate accumulator bounds;
  `mul32_sound` concludes exact 32×32→64 multiplication for every input pair.
  Each half product is at most 4294836225; both mixed accumulators fit u32.
- `mul32_widen` and `mul32_widen_limbs` identify that result with a widening
  multiply whose two inputs are 32-bit words, including explicit low/high
  extraction. This permits replacing the emitted partial-product helper by a
  native extended-u32 primitive without changing the field proofs. The theorem
  alone does not establish how a compiler lowers that widened expression; the
  generated ISA must establish that it uses native low/high u32 multiplication.
- `add_val`, `sub_val`, `lt_spec` and `mul_val` establish the generic word
  operations. `add_bits`, `sub_bits`, and `mul_bits` restate their refinement
  directly against the existing 64-bit bitvector operators.
- `assemble` is the existing scalar `glMul` schedule: add the high word of p00
  and low words of p01 and p10 into `middle`; pack p00.low and middle.low;
  add p11, p01.high, p10.high and middle.high for the high 64 bits. The middle
  accumulator can require 34 bits, so it remains a pair of u32 values. Its
  exactness and the final high accumulator's bound are proved, not assumed.
- `mulWide_sound` proves the complete 128-bit product. `mul_zeroExtend` equates
  the source network's generic 64-bit multiplication of zero-extended limbs
  with `mul32`. `mulNetwork_sound` therefore proves that precise four-product
  source schedule for every pair of 64-bit input words.
- `LimbsShift` proves `shr_bits` and `shl_bits` for all word values and counts,
  including zero, 32, 64 and nonzero high count limbs. The emitted helpers guard
  counts before shifting, since WGSL masks native shift counts. `lt_bits`,
  `mask32_bits`, `shr32_bits`, `shl32_bits` and `pack32_bits` connect the source
  network's comparisons, masks, shifts and packing to limb operations.

`Obend.Hardware.Gpu.LimbsGoldilocks` uses the same modulus, canonicalization and
reduction specs as the ZK application. `canon_val`, `add_val`, `sub_val` and
`reduce_val` refine those specifications. `reduceNetwork_eq_reduce` proves the
actual source reduction schedule: one canonicalization of the low word, then
canonical-input addition and subtraction. The intermediate bounds justify
those canonical-input preconditions. Finally, `Goldilocks.mul_sound` states
that this network equals `goldilocksMulSpec` for **all** input limbs, including
noncanonical inputs. It composes `mulNetwork_sound` and the existing
`goldilocksReduce128Spec_sound`; it is not a set of boundary-vector checks.

The original pure Nat definitions and proofs were moved byte-for-byte from
`Apps/ZkNtt.lean` to `Apps/ZkNtt/Goldilocks.lean`, preserving their namespace and
API. Both the application and the GPU refinement import that module. Arithmetic
proof builds no longer need the frontend, workbook machinery or hardware judge.

## Scope of the claim

The proved network is the arithmetic schedule in the shared `glMul` builder,
with its fixed masks and shifts identified by the bit-level lemmas above. This
is an arithmetic refinement and a representation refinement. It does not prove
by itself that executing the monadic wire builder or its ArrayGraph translation
produces this network, nor parse emitted WGSL or verify a shader compiler. The
existing graph-lowering theorem, source construction, emitted-code device gate
and ISA validation retain their respective responsibilities. A complete
builder-to-field theorem must compose those interfaces explicitly; it must not
be inferred merely from matching boundary vectors or matching source text.

No new claim about throughput, constant-time execution, or resource usage
follows from these arithmetic proofs. Those require the external gates and
measurements on the emitted kernels.

## Design references

The carry organization was checked against the actual local
[ec-gpu generated field implementation](https://github.com/emberian/ec-gpu/blob/efea2cf0d1d5dcb4506e1faee7ebec5ac48ec431/ec-gpu-gen/src/cl/field.cl)
and [ICICLE v1.1.0 field implementation](https://github.com/ingonyama-zk/icicle/blob/v1.1.0/icicle/primitives/field.cuh).
ICICLE's reduction uses multiprecision Barrett; ec-gpu provides Montgomery carry
chains. [Plonky3's Goldilocks reduction](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3/blob/main/goldilocks/src/goldilocks.rs)
uses the special shape of the prime. The comparison with Montgomery and Barrett
also used [Handbook of Applied Cryptography, chapter 14](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/hac/about/chap14.pdf),
Algorithms 14.32 and 14.42. Retaining the special-prime fold avoids introducing
Montgomery-domain conversions or reciprocal products into this ordinary-domain
Goldilocks workload. This is a design choice, not a measured cross-device
performance conclusion.
