# hardware/

What is here, and what is not.

- `sim/` — Verilator harness for the four modules the Lean compiler emits from `.obend`
  compositions (`module cleanroom_tcad_stack`, `module lc_drives_userspace_stator`, `module cnc_5axis_userspace_mill`)
  and from `Synth.goldenRetimedMacRtl` (`module obend_retimed_mac2`). Every `.sv` in `sim/` is
  regenerated by `lean/.lake/build/bin/obend-emit-sv` and compared byte-for-byte by
  `sim/verify_sim.sh`; none is edited by hand. `sim/obend_width_probe.sv` is
  `VerilateRunner.goldenWidthProbeRtl`: narrow signals declared `logic [w-1:0]`, slices, sign extension,
  truncation and concatenation, and two clock domains (`input clk_core`, `input clk_axi`) joined by a two-flop
  `ASYNC_REG` synchronizer; `verify_sim.sh` lints it with `verilator -Wall` and runs it through
  `obend verilate`.
- `ice40/` — the first physical target: an emitted module whose every signal has its width (16-bit
  product, addend and output registers, `checkWidths`-checked) placed and routed for the
  Lattice iCE40 HX1K on an iCEstick with the open toolchain (yosys, nextpnr-ice40, icestorm).
  `make` there produces a `.bin` bitstream; `make prog` loads it over `iceprog`.

- `fabulous/` — rung 5: `module obend_ice40_mac8` on an eFPGA generated by FABulous (pinned at `ba85d81`),
  and a Lean proof that the bitstream's logic realizes the module. `fabric.csv` is the fabric: 25 rows
  of one `W_IO` and three `LUT4AB` tiles (600 LUT4s, 50 pins; FABulous's IO tile library has only the
  west-edge `W_IO`, two pins per row, and the module has 49 port bits with `rst`). `top_wrapper.v` is the
  pin map (`obend fabric wrapper`). `flow.sh` runs FABulous's own flow (run_fab, Yosys
  `yosys -p synth_fabulous`, nextpnr-generic, FABulous's bit_gen) to `obend_ice40_mac8.bin` (235 LUT4s, 48 flops).
  `Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.Bitstream` decodes the `.bin` against FABulous's `bitStreamSpec.csv`,
  `pips.txt` and `bel.v2.txt` (on this design it agrees bit for bit with nextpnr's FASM) and follows
  the configured routing back to a `Net` of LUT4 gates and flops; `Mac8Net.lean` is that net, generated.
  `Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.mac8_fabric_realizes_module` (in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Fabulous/Mac8.lean`, from `fabric_realizes_module`, a plain
  induction) says the net shows `output mac_out` on every cycle of every in-width trace; its one-step and
  reset conditions are closed Boolean statements over the gate equations, discharged by `bv_decide`
  (compiled-evaluation trust, re-run by `#assert_compiled`). `verify_fabric.sh` rebuilds the bitstream
  byte for byte, regenerates `Mac8Net.lean` byte for byte, has `obend fabric check` accept the
  bitstream and refuse one with a LUT bit flipped (naming tile and bit), and simulates FABulous's
  fabric RTL configured with the bitstream (Icarus) against `moduleRun iceStickMac8`.
  `tile/` holds the LUT4AB tile model (`Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.Tile`): the switch matrix read from
  FABulous's generated `LUT4AB_switch_matrix.v` (one constructor per `models_pack.v` multiplexer;
  `LUT4ABSwitchMatrix.lean`, generated by `obend fabric tile`) and the two BELs transcribed from their
  Verilog, each an `obend-emit-sv` target. `lec_tile.sh` runs `hardware/lec/lec.sh` on each and then the
  same Lean half against Yosys's reading of FABulous's own tile Verilog (z3 `unsat`), and refuses a model
  with one switch-matrix table entry changed (`sat`). `verify_fabric.sh` also refuses a bitstream with a
  switch-matrix select bit of a used route flipped.
  `obend fabric tile-agree` checks every switch-matrix pip of every LUT4AB tile in `bitStreamSpec.csv`
  (99,675) against the model (`pipAgrees`) and every LUT4/MUX8LUT feature against the `ConfigBits`
  `LUT4AB.v` gives it (11,550); `Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.pip_sound` says an agreeing pip means the
  model's output is the pip's source whenever the decoder says so.
  The tile models meet: `smModule_eval_eq_SmPrim_eval` (`Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.TileEval`) says the
  LEC'd `smModule` computes `SmPrim.eval`, and `lut4c_O`/`lut4c_next` (`Cell`) read `lut4cModule` as
  Booleans. `Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.Readback`: `obend fabric cert` emits the readback cone certificate, a table with
  one entry per wire the extraction followed (6,143, chunked by tile, no wire twice), each with its driving
  hop and the entry it reads, ranked so the table is acyclic. Its equations (`ConeConsistent`: switch
  matrices through `smModule`, cells through `lut4cModule`, fixed wires, pads) are the cone's semantics,
  implied by the whole grid's (`Consistent.cone`). `consistent_exists_unique`: for every pin input and flop
  state the cone has exactly one assignment (`Cert.solve`), so loops through unused multiplexer inputs,
  outside the cone, do not make anything vacuous; `cone_sound` and `cone_run_eq`: every run of the cone
  shows `Net.run`, and `Cert.runFrom_run` gives one. `Cert.checkF` decides certificate validity (the table,
  switch matrices and frame positions read through balanced trees, names compared as numbers, each entry's
  path end from a witness checked one hop at a time; `Cert.checkF_sound`), and `binBits` (FABulous's `bit_gen`
  frame layout, header and frame-select words checked by `binFramed`) checks every bit the certificate requires (11,267).
  `Obend.Hardware.Fabulous.mac8_fabric_realizes_module_from_reset` (in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Fabulous/Mac8.lean`) is the
  verified MAC theorem over `mac8Net` (`lean/Obend/Hardware/Fabulous/Mac8Net.lean`), which `verify_fabric.sh`
  regenerates byte for byte from `obend_ice40_mac8.bin`. Taken as given: the tiles' top-level
  wiring (`LUT4AB.v`'s jump and nested wires, as `pips.txt` fixed pips, which `obend fabric check` checks),
  the configuration latches' index-to-frame map (`*_ConfigMem.v`), `binBits` as FABulous's bit_gen layout, and
  the IO pad as a pass-through.
  Not modelled, and refused by name if a bitstream uses them: the carry chain, `MUX8LUT`, the IO's
  registered output, block RAM, DSP and register-file tiles. The link from `.bin` to `mac8Net` is
  `obend fabric check` (compiled evaluation of the decoder, checking frame bits, fixed wires and tile types).

- `hvm/` — rung 2: Leaf books lowered to HVM2 interaction nets (`Obend.Hardware.Hvm`), run by HVM2
  itself (first-order bodies, records, sums, recursion). See below.

Removed (`review/EXPERT-fpga-silicon-20261103.md` §3.2, §3.3, §3.7): `aws-f2/`, whose shell
bridge declared ports the AWS F1/F2 shell does not expose; a second `sim/silicon_elab_top.sv` (the one

## LEC

`lec/lec.sh` is the logic-equivalence step (yosys, eqy and sby from the YosysHQ OSS CAD Suite under
`/Users/ember/tools/oss-cad-suite`, or `OSS_CAD`). Every `verify_*.sh` whose `.sv` is printed by
`emitModuleSV` from an `RtlModule` runs it after byte equality and before Verilator.

- **The emitted SV against Lean's model (all of them).** `obend-emit-sv <target> --lec` writes
  `X_lec.smt2` from the same `RtlModule` (`emitYosysLecSmt2` in `lean/Obend/Hardware/YosysLec.lean`; checked in,
  regenerated and byte-compared like the `.sv`). It is Lean's model of the module as `QF_BV` terms
  (`smtOf`, printed by `SmtBV.print`), read over the port and register names of Yosys's `yosys -p write_smt2` of
  `X.sv` (frontend and `proc` only, no optimization passes), and a miter. z3 `unsat` proves: for every input and every register state, the module
  Yosys reads from `X.sv` and Lean's `RtlModule` agree on every output (truncated to its port width)
  and on every register's next value, synchronous resets included. With registers matched by name
  that is sequential equivalence from any common state. Nothing between the two halves is
  hand-written. z3 decides it bit-precisely within `LEC_SECS` (60 s) or, failing that, with every
  multiplication of width W in both halves replaced by one free function `(lec_mul_W a b)`; `unsat` then
  holds for every interpretation of multiplication, so for the real one (the risc core needs this:
  bit-precise, its register-file writeback through two 64-bit multiplies did not finish in 20
  minutes; abstracted it is `unsat` within two minutes). A `sat` that appears only under the abstraction is
  reported as undecided and fails the step.

  The whole Lean half is the print of one Lean value, `lecScript m : LecScript`: the two Yosys states
  and their assertions, the in_i and r_k read through Yosys's accessors (zero-extended from the
  port or register width; 1-bit signals are `Bool` in Yosys), the wires, every output and register
  obligation at its declared width (the reset wrapper `(ite rst init next)` included), and the
  miter. What is proved (`lean/Obend/Hardware/YosysLec.lean`):
  - `lecScript_holds_iff`: for every assignment z3 may choose, the script holds exactly when both
    Yosys assertions hold and some output Yosys reads in `s`, or some register it reads in `s1`,
    differs at its width from Lean's step on the inputs and registers it reads in `s`. So `unsat`
    means every one-step reading agrees with Lean, and a `sat` is a real disagreement.
  - `lecScript_unsat_of_step`: for a one-domain module with distinct register indices, that
    agreement is stated against the module's own semantics, `evalModuleOutputsW` and
    `stepModuleRegs` (equal to `stepModuleDomain` with one domain).
  - `moduleFaithful_of_checkWidths`: the emission gate `checkRtlModuleW` implies the theorems'
    hypothesis; `obend-emit-sv --lec` refuses exactly what the `.sv` emission refuses.

  Which clocking it checks: Yosys's single global clock, every flip-flop latching on every
  transition, with each register reset by its own domain's `rst_<d>`. For a module with several
  clock domains (`module obend_width_probe`) that is all clocks rising together; the edge-by-edge
  semantics of `stepModuleDomain`, where one domain latches and the others hold, is not checked.

  Trusted: Yosys's SystemVerilog frontend and `yosys -p write_smt2`; z3; and that the two print functions,
  `LecScript.print` and `SmtBV.print`, write the SMT-LIB syntax of what they print.
  `lean/tests/silicon_elab/YosysLecTest.lean` pins them (every expression constructor against
  `emitExprSmt2`, the glue lines of two committed files verbatim, two whole committed files byte
  for byte); that is a test, not a proof. Read into the model, not proved: narrow ports and
  registers are zero-extended into the 64-bit carrier.
- **The emitted SV against its own gate-level netlist (EQY, where it terminates).** `synth` makes
  `X.gl.v`; EQY proves gold `X.sv` equivalent to gate `X.gl.v`, matching ports and flip-flops.
  This says synthesis preserved what Yosys read; it says nothing about Lean by itself, but composed
  with the LEC it puts the gate netlist, not only the SV, equal to the `RtlModule`. It runs for
  `module cleanroom_tcad_stack`, `module lc_drives_userspace_stator`, `module cnc_5axis_userspace_mill`,
  `module obend_width_probe` and `module sovereign_drex_batch_top`. It does not
  run for modules with a 64-bit variable multiply (`module obend_retimed_mac2`, risc, zk, fidget, quantum):
  SAT-based equivalence of a word-level multiply against a gate-level multiplier does not terminate
  (mac2's `a * b` partition ran for five minutes without an answer).
- **Gates.** Each run flips bit 0 of the first output in a copy of `X.sv` (never the committed file);
  the LEC must answer `sat` and prints the failing obligation with both values, and EQY must fail.

App-level sequential LEC coverage: `apps/foc-hil/verilator/foc_drive_top.sv` is emitted from
`Obend.Apps.FocHil.focHilSequentialRtlModule` (`obend-emit-sv foc-hil`) and LEC-checked against
`foc_drive_top_lec.smt2` (`--no-eqy`, 64-bit Q32 decoupling multiplies); `apps/sdr-ft8/verilator/`
emits and LEC-checks the three receive-chain stages `sdr_nco_mix.sv`, `sdr_cic3_r64.sv` and
`sdr_ft8_disc.sv` from `sdrNcoModule`, `sdrCicModule` and `sdrFt8DiscModule` in
`lean/Obend/Apps/SdrFt8Rx.lean`. Outside `RtlModule` LEC: `apps/sdr-ft8/verilator/sdr_ft8_top.sv`
(legacy hand-written transceiver reference) and `silicon-kernel-verilator` (its `.sv` is printed by
a string emitter, not from an `RtlModule`).

Two standalone Z3 `QF_BV` property sheets, `apps/foc-hil/verilator/foc_drive_props.smt2` and
`apps/sdr-ft8/verilator/sdr_ft8_props.smt2`, run inside their app `verify_*.sh` scripts: the FOC
sheet checks safety-barrier formulas alongside the `foc_drive_top.sv` `RtlModule` LEC, and the SDR
sheet checks formulas transcribed from the hand-written `sdr_ft8_top.sv` reference datapath. All
other app hardware modules (`zk-ntt`, `fidget-cam`, `quantum-photonic-matter`,
`sovereign-drex`) are emitted directly from an `RtlModule` and verified on their `.sv` by `lec.sh`.

`rust-ffi/` is unrelated to the FPGA path (CBOR codec, FEMM/octree engines) and is owned by the
physics lane.

## `hvm/`: a Leaf book on HVM2 (rung 2)

HVM2 (HigherOrderCO, crate `hvm` 2.0.22, paper `paper/HVM2.typst` in its repository) is the target,
not HVM3: HVM3 is a work-in-progress Haskell runtime for the Interaction Calculus (a term language)
with no paper, no net IR and no GPU backend, while HVM2 runs a documented net language on Rust, C and
CUDA runtimes. `Obend.Hardware.Hvm` (and `Hvm.Model`, `Hvm.Net`, `Hvm.Lower`) holds a model of that
language (nodes, `operate`, the interaction rules as a relation `Step`), the lowering `lowerBookToHvm`,
the emitter `emitHvm`, and `lowerBookToHvm_sound`: for an accepted book, a lowered member, arguments,
and a fuel at which every result slot evaluates exactly below `2^24`, the model reduces `@main` to
the tuple of the slot values with an empty bag, and each observation the result's sort names (the
scalar, a record's fields, a sum's tag and payload) holds of `evalBookAt`'s term under Core4's
reference reduction. The module header names where the model departs from HVM2.

`Hvm.Inet` maps these nets to `ReductionMachine`'s arena for the combinator fragment (`CON`, `DUP`,
`ERA`). `ReductionMachine.Arena` gives the machine an arena semantics: node memory and a link table,
`Arena.Step` firing an active pair by the outcome `reduceActivePair` gives it. `Repr A ρ c` says the
arena is the configuration, and `inetOfHvm_sound` that every VOID, ERAS, ANNI and COMM step of a
represented configuration is one `Arena.Step` to an arena representing the result (after HVM2's LINK
of the bare wires ANNI leaves), when the fired pair's children share no variable. The machine's
commutation is Lafont's since 2026-11-05 (`commuteSpawnedNodes`, `commuteSplices`;
`commute_wellformed`: every port of the result is on exactly one wire; the old shape, which put a
slot on two wires, is kept as a `#tooth` mutation). `NUM`, `REF`, `OPR` and `SWI` are not mapped.

The fragment: first-order bodies over naturals and Booleans, unboxed records of scalars (a `CON`
tuple at a member's result), sums whose arms carry a scalar or a record (a tag and the widest
payload), `case`, recursion through `fix (λrec. λsup. λx. body)` applied to one argument (one HVM2
definition per fixpoint, recursive calls as `REF`s, branches that call lifted to definitions so the
untaken one is voided before it expands; `Nat.rec` as Lean's lowering emits it is of this shape), and
λ only as a method's curried result. Refused by name: labels, the object forms, activities, `split`,
the bitwise and fixed-point primitives, λ as a value anywhere else, recursion returning a record or
a sum.

`verify_hvm.sh` regenerates `arith.hvm`, `rec.hvm` and `data.hvm` and compares bytes, checks the book
`refused` is refused, runs every vector of every book under `hvm run` and `hvm run-c` against the
slots `evalBookAt` shows (each probe run on the demand machine) and against the model's own run, and
requires each planted emitter rule (`--plant swapSwitch`, `--plant addAsSub`; `verify_hvm.sh --plant
NAME` runs one alone) to make each book's comparison fail. Build `lake build obend-emit-hvm` in
`lean/` first; HVM2 from `cargo install hvm` or `HVM_BIN`. `hvm run-cu` needs an NVIDIA GPU; hbox's GPU is
AMD and HVM2 has no HIP backend, so the CUDA runtime stays unrun here.

## `gpu/`: open hypergraphs on a GPU (rung G1 of `review/GPU-PROGRAM-20261105.md`)

`Obend.Hardware.ArrayHypergraph` is the scalar `StrictOpenHypergraph` with wires carrying vectors of
64-bit words: a hyperedge is an elementwise program (instructions `dst := op args` over names that are
wires, the `HypergraphOp`s plus the bit operations a Goldilocks multiply needs; a graph as built has one
instruction per map edge), a gather along a `FiniteFunction` (Wilson–Zanasi's permutation datum), a
concatenation or a constant array. The scalar graphs are the one-element instance: `ofScalar` lifts every
hyperedge to a map over length-one wires and `ofScalar_layered_sound` says the array evaluation of the
image is the scalar 64-bit evaluation, wire by wire. `evalGraphArr` is the same evaluation with the wires
stored as arrays and a program's locals in a hash map (`evalGraphArr_eq`); the closure-based model,
compiled, re-evaluated an edge on every read and took exponential time in dependency depth, so the emitter
computes expected values with the array form.

Two passes sit between the graph and the shaders. `fuseGraph` is Halide's `compute_inline` and TVM's
elementwise fusion: every map edge recomputes the elementwise producers of its sources (their instructions
precede its own, their sources become its sources), and a producer read by nothing but map edges and not
an output is dropped; `fuseGraph_sound` says the fused graph has the same outputs on a well-formed graph
(`ArrayGraph.wf`: every edge passes `edgeOk`, destinations written once and after every read of them, map
edges primitive) and its edges still pass `edgeOk`. `layerEdges` is the frontier algorithm of
Wilson–Zanasi's `open-hypergraphs` crate (`strict/graph.rs`, `kahn`) over the hazard relation: a stage is
the edges whose predecessors have all been dispatched; `layerGraph_sound` says every stage is pairwise
independent, the stages flatten to a permutation of the edges, and the synchronous stage-by-stage
evaluation is the single-pass one (independent edges commute, `stepEdge_comm`). The scalar module's
greedy `layerHyperEdges` stays there as its own.

`Obend.Hardware.Gpu` lowers fused, layered graphs using an Array-backed last-use allocator.
Dead fused wires receive no storage; disjoint live intervals reuse storage, and an eligible final-use
map may overwrite its input. `layoutOk` checks the actual allocation, including per-stage read/write
aliasing. `lowerArrayToGpu_sound` is universal over well-formed graphs with that check accepted and an
initial representation of live values; its invariant follows liveness through every stage.
`lowerArrayToGpu_raceFree` includes safe in-place maps. A concatenation uses conditional loads rather
than eager `select`, so inactive branches cannot race with an in-place writer.

`Obend.Hardware.ArrayKernel` extends the source vocabulary with indexed multi-output maps, device,
constant and workgroup buffers, register programs, and barrier-separated phases. A phase runs a
strided loop over logical lanes; every physical lane reaches the uniform workgroup and storage
barriers. There is no NTT primitive. `Program.check` checks bounds (including u32 intermediate
arithmetic), initialization, register SSA, unique scatters and device read/write ownership.
`BlockLower.checkedProgram_sound` proves its separately defined flat-memory target represents the
source evaluation for every accepted program. Expected values use Array-backed storage, compact
pending writes and hash-map registers, rather than reevaluating a functional graph on each read.

`Gpu.Workgroup` maps the actual accesses to the existing G4 Vulkan model. Mandatory
`SourceProtocol.checkProgram` checks arithmetic-free synchronization protocols using HashSets.
`DispatchProtocol.checkProgram_not_racy` composes every accepted group into a race-free dispatch;
`checkProgram_read_sequential` derives phase visibility in every well-formed consistent execution.
These are universal theorems with executable admission hypotheses, not a fixture enumeration.
G4's initial-zero convention does not model arbitrary host uploads; that remains an explicit boundary.

Ordinary array programs retain the lexical Leaf/Core4 front. `ArrayMember.checkMember` checks
member syntax and scope, and `ArrayTyping.checkTypedGraph` returns typing and live-layout receipts.
`Plan.source_to_gpu_encoded` composes `book_sound`, source compilation and general GPU lowering.
Field programs structurally reify the shared `ArrayBuild`/`GoldilocksSource` builder, rather than
maintaining another field arithmetic implementation. Reductions and scans retain segment-owned
linear invocation loops, now emitted using the same limb representation as maps.

The frontend accepts exported scalar book files and serialized integer catgrad models.
[`gpu/SOURCE.md`](gpu/SOURCE.md) describes the book workflow;
[`gpu/catgrad/README.md`](gpu/catgrad/README.md) describes the upstream MLP and explicit integer adaptation;
[`gpu/SOAC.md`](gpu/SOAC.md) describes segmented folds and their current sequential schedule.
The lexical fragment does not express workgroup-indexed register networks, shared-memory phases or
barriers. The sole NTT builder is `Gpu.NttKernel`, in the block language described below; the lexical
encoded-source theorem does not claim to cover it.

The emitted scalar representation is `vec2<u32>`. `Gpu.Limbs`, `LimbsShift` and
`LimbsGoldilocks` prove carry, product, shift and Goldilocks reduction refinements for arbitrary limbs.
The bounded 32×32 widening primitive currently uses Naga/wgpu's SHADER_INT64 syntax; RADV lowers it
to native `v_mul_lo_u32`/`v_mul_hi_u32`. General emulated 64×64 shader multiplication is removed.
See [the arithmetic proof boundary](../review/GPU-LIMBS-20261106.md).

`Gpu.NttKernel` builds Stockham NTTs in that general source language: radix-4 register butterflies,
multiple shared-memory phases, balanced uploads, and packed small tiles. The 1,024 / 65,536 / 1,048,576
point transforms use 3 / 2 / 3 dispatches. Each input vector is canonicalized in registers. The emitter
checks source evaluation against an independent recursive NTT and, through 1,024 points, a direct DFT.
That is a mathematical algorithm check, not a universal NTT-construction theorem.
The old per-layer NTT graph builder and its superseded shader stages are removed.

`verify_gpu.sh` checks regenerated bytes, exact device outputs, planted `addAsSub`, `swapMux` and
`mulHighZero` faults, then extracts real RADV machine code with resource statistics. The two largest
NTTs use committed SHA-256 manifests instead of large plans/oracles. `gpu/runner --bench 1024`
pre-records a timestamped batch, resets input outside each timestamp interval, and checks the final
output. The [named Plonky3 baseline](gpu/baseline/README.md) reports all three requested sizes,
CPU thread configurations, device distributions and separate amortized host batch time.
The [resource model](gpu/cost/README.md) predicts Mesa static occupancy from observed allocations;
a real LDS-pressure shader changes both reported resources and that prediction. It does not yet
constitute the full formal G5 grading.

Current integration evidence and open ISA-model instructions are recorded in
[GPU-KERNELS-20261106](../review/GPU-KERNELS-20261106.md). Earlier RADV/AMDVLK/Metal passes applied
to the superseded shaders and do not certify this revision. The new workgroup shaders require the
RDNA2 model lane to add lane-count and shared-memory instructions before the full G2 gate is green.
The array-to-RTL commuting square below covers ordinary array graphs; no block-NTT-to-RTL theorem is claimed.

### rung G2: the dumped ISA, read by Lean (`gpu/rdna2/`, `isa_check.sh`)

`gpu/rdna2/amdgpu_isa_rdna2.xml` is AMD's machine-readable RDNA 2 specification (GPUOpen, bundle of 2026-08-06;
`gpu/rdna2/README.md` has the URL, date and hashes). `gpu/rdna2/gen_encodings.py` writes
`Obend.Hardware.Gpu.Rdna2.Encodings` and `Insts.*` from it: every encoding with its identifier mask,
identifiers, condition expressions and bit fields, every instruction with every encoding entry and
operand, every operand type with its predefined values; nothing in them is typed. `Obend.Hardware.Gpu.Rdna2.Decode`
reads a dword stream as GPUOpen's `IsaDecoder` does (mask and identifiers in `Order`, the opcode, then
the XML's condition picks the literal/DPP/SDWA variant and its width) and renders the instruction in
LLVM's syntax. `isa_extract.py` now keeps the machine code RADV prints after each instruction and, with
`RADV_DEBUG=shaderstats`, the resource line per stage (VGPRs, SGPRs, LDS, scratch, code size: every stage
carries VGPR, SGPR and LDS allocations consumed by the empirical cost model).

`Obend.Hardware.Gpu.Rdna2.Semantics` is the wave64 model of the instructions RADV emits for our kernels:
SGPRs, VCC, EXEC, SCC, M0, the VGPRs per lane, a dword program counter, dword memory and an error field,
after LNSym's `ArmState` (accessors, a `step` that decodes and executes, a `run`), with the stores as
arrays so the compiled check runs the largest dump in seconds. Each instruction follows the
pseudo-code of the RDNA 2 ISA reference guide (chapter 12, cited per instruction) and, where the
instruction exists in gem5's executable Vega model, was checked against gem5's `execute` body:
`gpu/rdna2/gem5_ref.cpp` ports those bodies line by line, `gpu/rdna2/gem5_ref.py` assembles each twin with
llvm-mc and writes `gpu/rdna2/gem5_vectors.tsv` (random register states before and after), and the model's
`step` must reproduce every register of every row. The subset: `s_mov_b32/b64`, `s_movk_i32`,
`s_and_b64`, `s_cbranch_execz`, `s_endpgm`, `s_waitcnt`, `s_clause`, `s_load_dword*`,
`s_buffer_load_dword*`, `buffer_load/store_dword{,x2,x4}`, `v_mov_b32`, `v_add_nc_u32`, `v_and/or_b32`,
`v_lshlrev_b32`, `v_lshl_add_u32`, `v_add3_u32`, `v_and_or_b32`, `v_mul_lo/hi_u32`, `v_cndmask_b32`, `v_add/sub_co_u32`,
`v_add/sub_co_ci_u32`, `v_lshlrev/lshrrev_b64`, `v_cmp_le/gt_u32`, `v_cmpx_gt_u32`, `v_cmp_lt_u64`,
`v_cmp_ne_i64`; everything else, every operand code outside the integer and register codes, VOP3
modifiers on integer operations and swizzled or indexed buffers are refused by the model, not guessed.
`S_CLAUSE` and the `v_*_co_ci` and `v_add_nc` names have no Vega twin and rest on the manual; `V_CMPX`
writes EXEC only here where Vega's also wrote VCC (the vectors exclude VCC for it). The universal
statements are at the instruction level: `mulHi_mulLo` (the two halves of the 64-bit product, which
`glmul-256` and the NTTs chain), `addCo_spec`, `addCi_spec`, `subCo_spec` (the carries are exactly the
dropped bits).

The correspondence is a certificate, checked once per dump, that RADV's machine code computes the
graph for every input (translation validation: Necula, PLDI 2000; Alive2, PLDI 2021; the symbolic
simulation is LNSym's approach on our own model). The model is generic over its value and memory domains
and every instruction commutes with domain homomorphisms (`run_hom` in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Natural.lean`), so one `step` runs both
concretely and over terms (`Term`: hash-consed 32-bit terms with folding constructors and their
evaluation laws), and `wave_sound` (in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Sym.lean`) relates a symbolic wave to every concrete wave it describes.
`Host` is the memory RADV binds (`ac_build_raw_buffer_descriptor`, Mesa 24.2.8; the set's address in
`s2`, the workgroup id in `s3`, the thread id in `v0`, per `radv_shader_args.c` and the manual's 3.12).
What is certified is the schedule the emitter dispatches (`ArrayEmit.compile` of the lowering): an
elementwise stage of the plan, or a collective, an admitted block program whose dispatches run LDS
phases between barriers. Each dispatch is a certificate stage (`Spec` in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Cert.lean`: its threads, its LDS, a
table of the words it writes as terms over the dispatch's start, its meaning), an elementwise stage's
table the plan's kernels in 32-bit limbs (`pKernel`, `pKernel_sound` in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Lower.lean`) and a block dispatch's the
dispatch run symbolically group by group and phase by phase as `BlockLower` runs it (`Block.blockSpec`).
`checkProgram` (in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Cert.lean`) runs every wave of every dispatch symbolically from a start memory whose wire buffer
is a variable per dword (constants an earlier dispatch stored are kept as literals; the LDS is per
workgroup scratch the start view never reads) and checks each stored dword: in the buffer, in a word the
dispatch's table names, stored once, and equal to the table's term up to a residual. EXEC is a pair of
values like any register: a lane whose bit a compare computed from data writes the select, on that bit,
of its new and old value (manual 6.2.2), and an `S_CBRANCH_EXECZ` on such a mask is a join
(`srun` in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Join.lean`): the region is run once symbolically and once with EXEC zero, and when the second leaves
every VGPR and stored dword as it was, the join keeps the first's vector state and selects each changed
scalar on EXECZ, the reading by which LLVM drops or keeps the branch (`mustRetainExeczBranch`), with the
state merge of symbolic execution; `srun_sound`: the concrete run, which branches by its own EXEC, ends
where the merged run says. The terms meet by normal forms (`Term`: sums flattened and ordered, more than
64 addends as a treap keyed by hash with hash-derived priorities, which is uniquely represented, so one
multiset of addends is one term however it was grouped and a running sum's next value shares all but
one path with it; `a - b` as `a + (0 - b)`, a select's condition with its
`≠ 0` wrappers removed and shared addends moved out of it, symmetric compares ordered). `Equiv`
cuts the two terms at memory words and their maximal common subterms (cutpoints, as in combinational
equivalence checking), never at literals, and when a sample environment refutes the cut residual (a cut
hid a relation the sides need) at the memory words alone; what is left is a small `Residual` over leaf
variables, and
the residuals every dump needs are one generated module, `Residuals`, each proved for every memory by
`bv_decide`, by `grind` where it multiplies, or, for a 64-bit product the compiler regrouped
(`hi(a·(y+y))` against `hi(a·y + a·y)`), by lifting to 64-bit words (`Lift`: the limb lowering's
evaluation law, then `grind`'s ring solver). `Schedule.certifySchedule_sound`: if the certificate
accepts and the residuals hold, then for every content of the wire buffer the decoded programs, run on
the model as the host dispatches the schedule (the index buffer `ArrayEmit.scheduleTables`, the function
the plan's tables are written from), leave in every word what the schedule's actions compute
(`ArrayEmit.evalActions`). `certifyDump` (in `lean/Obend/Hardware/Gpu/Rdna2/Certified.lean`) is the check of one dump: the graph well formed with a
checked layout, the waits sufficient (`Waitcnt`, below: the hypothesis that makes the model's immediate
memory the device's), every stage decoded, and the schedule certified with the library;
`certifyDump_sound` composes it with `ArrayEmitCorrect.compile_runProgram` and `lowerArrayToGpu_sound`,
so the output wires are `evalGraph`'s, for every input. The NTT, a block program alone, is certified the
same way by `certifyBlockDump` (`certifyBlock_eval`: every device element is `evalProgram`'s). Both rest
on the standard axioms and the compiled `bv_decide` lemmas. Workgroups of a dispatch are run in order;
that is sound because a block dispatch's table refuses a word two groups write and a group's read of a
word another group of the dispatch wrote (the race freedom `DispatchProtocol.checkProgram_not_racy`
states for admitted sources, checked here on the machine code's own terms). The check itself runs the
waves of a dispatch on their own tasks: a block dispatch gives its table in parts, one group's words
each (`Spec.part`), never held whole (the 2^20-point NTT's dispatch table alone held 46 GB); a first pass
guesses each word's owner, and every wave rebuilds its part from the owners, checks that it holds
exactly the words they give it, and runs with every dword of an earlier part's word unreadable, which
covers every store before it (`mergeWaves_sound`); a stage without parts runs each wave with every word
of its table unreadable (`mergeWavesT_sound`) and falls back to the waves in order. The merge in wave
order is the dispatch's bookkeeping, so the result is the sequential check's.
`Obend.Teeth.Hardware.Rdna2Runs` pins it with `native_decide` and `#assert_compiled` on every graph but
the 65536- and 2^20-point NTTs and the two 65536-element SOACs (the gate certifies those), with the gem5
vectors. `Check` keeps the concrete run from `expected.tsv`'s
vectors as a smoke test of the model and the layout.

`isa_check.sh` is the gate, on hbox: the generated modules are byte-identical to the generator's output;
the Lean decoder's text equals `llvm-mc --disassemble` (LLVM 19, gfx1031, wave64) on every instruction of
every dump; every bit of every instruction's machine code, flipped, changes the decoding or fails it
unless it lies in no field the specification names; diamond's programs give `expected.tsv` on the model
and a planted flip in a store's register is caught; the gem5 vectors are the generator's bytes and
replay; the waits are sufficient and a removed wait is refused; `Residuals.lean` is the generator's
bytes, every dump is certified (each with the check's time and peak memory) and a planted flip in a
store's register is refused. Passed on hbox 2026-11-08 on all 29 dumps (stages 1-7; stage 7 in 3391 s with
four certificates at a time, the 2^20-point NTT in 1467-3072 s and 1.6 GB, the 65536-element SOACs in
273-590 s), `Rdna2Runs` built.

`Obend.Hardware.Gpu.Rdna2.Waitcnt` closes the gap the model's immediate memory leaves: the model
completes an access when it issues, so a program that read a load's destination before waiting for it
would be right here and stale on the device. The module walks every dumped program as a dataflow over
its blocks, tracking the outstanding vector and scalar loads with the manual's counter rules (4.4, 7.3:
vector loads return in order, scalar loads out of order so only `lgkmcnt(0)` completes them) and the
hazard rules of LLVM's `SIInsertWaitcnts` (every use and def against the counter that scores it; a
vector load's write-after-write needs no wait on gfx10; store data needs no wait), and refuses a program
that touches a load's destination before a covering wait. `isa_check.sh` stage 6 runs it on every dump
and requires a dump with the first `vmcnt` wait of stage 0 replaced by `s_nop 0` to be refused;
`gpu/rdna2/waitcnt_plant.py` plants the same `s_nop` in the machine code the card runs, through Mesa's
shader cache, for the measurement reported in `gpu/rdna2/README.md`.

What the model does not yet do: execute the new shared-memory workgroup kernels. Its sequential-wave
execution covered the earlier disjoint stage kernels; the workgroup extension must preserve G4's
scoped communication rules and add the instructions recorded in the current lane report.

### `gpu/litmus/`: the memory model, measured (rung G4)

`Obend.Hardware.Gpu.Memory` is the memory model of WGSL as the Vulkan memory model specialized to the
events WGSL emits (its header derives the specialization: relaxed atomics at QueueFamily or Workgroup scope
by address space, barriers at Workgroup scope with the semantics of one address space, everything
available and visible at workgroup scope, so `locord = loc ∩ hb` and happens-before never crosses a
workgroup), presented as sections of a presheaf on the scope site: a section over a thread, a workgroup or
the device is a witness (reads-from, coherence ranks, a total order every edge of `locord ∪ rf ∪ fr ∪ asmo`
respects) of the shape restricted to that object, and an outcome is allowed when it is the restriction of
a section over the device. `allowed_iff_glues` says the enumeration-based checker answers exactly that.
The referent is not our reading: `lean/tools/litmuscheck` (`obend-litmus-check`) writes every shape and
outcome as a Dartagnan Vulkan litmus test and the gate requires Dartagnan's verdict under `cat/vulkan.cat`
to equal the checker's on each (it does, on every outcome of the suite).

`gpu/litmus/` is GPUHarbor's suite (`wgsl/`, copied with attribution) run by `obend-gpu-litmus` (a port of
its host side and of its tuned-stress parameter space, in `gpu/runner`) on hbox's RX 6750 XT under RADV
and AMDVLK, hbox's UHD 770 under ANV and the Mac's M2 Max under Metal; the tables are under
`gpu/litmus/results/` with their seed and budget, and `results/STRONGER.tsv` records the allowed weak
behaviours no device showed. The relaxed weak behaviours at device scope appear on the AMD and Apple
parts, IRIW's only on the M2 Max, none on the Intel part; a `storageBarrier` across workgroups
leaves them in place on the M2 Max, as the model says it may; no device showed a coherence violation, a
weak outcome of a workgroup-scope test with barriers, or a weak outcome of the non-atomic barrier tests.
The shapes are each thread's whole program with every barrier of the kernel body in place (a thread plays
both roles of GPUHarbor's pseudo-code), which forbids the `seq0` class of the workgroup barrier tests as
well as their weak class; no device showed `seq0` either. `verify_litmus.sh` is the gate (`gpu/litmus/README.md`).

The kernel lane adds checked live layouts, workgroup source programs and an empirical resource model
(`gpu/cost/`). The formal G5 grading remains open; current evidence is in
[GPU-KERNELS-20261106](../review/GPU-KERNELS-20261106.md).

### `gpu/square/`: the same graph through two devices (rung G1s)

`Obend.Hardware.Square` is the square the council asked for, done with external runs: one open hypergraph
lowered to the GPU program above and to a combinational `RtlModule`, each leg run outside Lean on the
same vectors, and theorems that the two lowerings denote one evaluation.

One lowering. `compileArrayGraphToRtl` is single assignment: module wire `i` is the `i`-th SSA
definition (input elements, then accumulator temporaries and destination elements), and an array
of slots (`LowerSt`) records, per array wire, where its current definition starts. A reader is lowered
against the definition current at that point, which is what `stepEdge` reads, so the graph's own wire
numbering, a wire defined twice and an edge reading its own destination all lower faithfully: the only
hypothesis is `ArrayGraph.wf`. A map's `ElemProg` is run symbolically into one expression per element
(`progExpr`; the shifts are exact for every amount, `evalRtlExpr_elemOpToRtl`), a gather is wiring, a
concatenation renaming, a constant constants. `RtlFold.compile` gives folds shared accumulator
wires; scan outputs reuse prefixes, so circuit size is linear, with sequential depth.
`RtlFold.compile_sound` proves equality with `evalFold`, including empty segments.
The lowering takes the graph before fusion; the GPU lowering
fuses (`fuseGraph`); the theorems meet at `evalGraph`. A scalar graph is the one-element instance,
`compileHypergraphToRtl g = compileArrayGraphToRtl (ofScalar g)`, as `lowerHypergraphToGpu` is on the
GPU side; the scalar lowering this replaced needed every wire defined once as a side condition and failed
the emission check on the adjoint graphs, whose wires are numbered out of definition order. The three
operation lowerings of `Obend.Hardware.OpenHypergraph` are one, `compileHypergraphOpExpr`, which the
pipelined compiler and `elemOpToRtl` share.

The theorems. `compileArrayGraphToRtl_sound`: for every well-formed array graph and every input, the
module's output ports are `evalGraph` from the environment the input ports induce (`inputEnvOf`),
flattened. `rtl_eq_gpu_array`: the GPU program, run from any buffer that holds that environment at its
checked layout (`layoutOk`) with the live-entry representation (`Flat`), shows the same words. `compileHypergraphToRtl_sound` and `rtl_eq_gpu` are the scalar
corollaries, against the scalar single pass `evalHyperEdgesCombBv`. `compileHypergraphToRtlPipelined_sound`
is the pipelined compiler's whole module (one clock edge per wavefront stage with the inputs held shows the
layered evaluation, from any register state), and `pipelined_eq_comb` puts it at a third corner. The
diamond-specific pipelined theorems it replaces are gone; the diamond's `87` is a `#guard` in
`tests/metasystem/NextMathematicaSoundnessTest.lean`.

The graphs (`squarePlan` in `Obend.Hardware.SquareGraphs`, the one list both emitters share), each for
what no other one exercises:

| graph | for |
|---|---|
| `allops` | every scalar operation: a negative constant, add, sub, mul, scale by `-2^40`, mac, mux, with wrap-around |
| `muxchain` | a multiply that reaches an output only through a mux's test against zero, so its planted swap is seen only by a vector with a zero operand (the scalar vectors include four one-hot ones for this) |
| `adjoint-square-bilinear` | a `compileGraphAdjoint` graph, wires numbered out of definition order: the single-assignment lowering's reason |
| `tree512` | size: 511 chained adders, where the LEC's time goes |
| `glmul-4` | the bit operations of the Goldilocks multiply (ult, and, or, both shifts) as elementwise maps over arrays |
| `array-route` | gathers, concatenation and constant arrays with a shared producer |
| `soac-boundaries` | segmented reductions/scans, empty segments, modular monoids and producer/consumer composition |

The gate, `verify_square.sh`, per graph: the committed `sv/<top>.sv` regenerates byte for byte;
`obend-emit-sv square-<graph> --expected`, the HDL model's `expected.tsv` from `evalModuleOutputs`, equals
`../graphs/<graph>/expected.tsv`, the array model's, byte for byte; `hardware/lec/lec.sh` (the LEC of
Yosys's reading against Lean's model and its planted bit flip; EQY against the synthesized netlist for the
graphs in `EQY_GRAPHS`); the Verilator harness `obend-emit-sv square-<graph> --tb`
(`emitVerilatorHarnessCpp`, the harness `obend verilate` uses, over the graph's vectors against
`evalModuleOutputs`) reports no mismatch on the committed `.sv`; `obend-gpu-run` agrees with
`expected.tsv` on the card; and a swapped operator in a copy of the `.sv` (`+` to `-`, else `*` to `+`,
trying operators nearest the outputs first) makes the LEC answer sat and Verilator report mismatches. A
swap the LEC proves equivalent, or cannot decide within its limits (`lec.sh`'s `LEC_ABS_SECS`, set by the
tooth, bounds the query with multiplication uninterpreted, under which a sat reads only as undecided), is
skipped and counted. `YOSYS_BIN` and `VERILATOR_BIN` select the executables. The gate removes
`VERILATOR_BIN` from the child environment because Verilator reserves it for its internal binary.

The shared LEC retains SMT expression sharing through the installed Yosys `smtio.py` unroller
and defining equalities. Model definitions precede `check-sat`; solver errors are rejected.
Exact bounded SAT witnesses speed the negative checks without letting constrained UNSAT prove
equivalence. A planted bit flip requires exact SAT. `YOSYS_SMTIO` selects a nonstandard unroller.
The obsolete per-layer NTT square fixture is replaced by `array-route`; `soac-boundaries` remains.
The block NTT is covered by its own lowering and device gates, not the array-RTL theorem.
EQY remains opt-in; LEC is the default equivalence step.
